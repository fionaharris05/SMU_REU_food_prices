# SMU_REU_food_prices

## Research Question
Does a healthier diet cost more and become less affordable?

## Why I chose this topic
I was inspired by previous courses I have taken in Nutrition Science, where I learned about the effects of food deserts and the affordability of healthy food on different populations. It is well known that some locations and populations face greater economic barriers to accessing nutritious food. I wanted to explore this issue further by comparing the price and affordability differences globally between three types of diets: an energy-sufficient diet, a nutrient-adequate diet, and a healthy diet.

## Data Used
I used data from the Food Prices for Nutrition data hub published by the World Bank. This dataset includes information on food prices and affordability from over 190 countries.
For this project, I focused on variables related to the cost and affordability of three diet types: Energy-sufficient, Nutrient-adequate, and Healthy.

## Major Findings
My analysis showed that the cost of food generally increases when moving from an energy-sufficient diet to a nutrient-adequate diet and then to a healthy diet. However, affordability is not only about the price of food. It also reflects broader economic conditions, including income, inequality, and regional access to nutritious foods.
The results showed that Africa and parts of Asia are substantially disadvantaged in terms of accessing healthier and more nutrient-dense foods compared with many countries in the Western Hemisphere. This suggests that the affordability of healthy diets is closely connected to larger economic disparities, rather than just the price of the food itself. These findings highlight that countries should monitor the affordability of healthy diets and support programs that improve access to nutritious foods, especially for populations facing economic disadvantage.
